What we take on

Most plumbing calls are small jobs that have been put off. They are worth doing before they become big ones: a dripping tap wastes water and wears its seat, and a weeping joint under a floor will eventually find the ceiling below.

Leaks and burst pipes

Tracing the source, repairing or replacing the section, and checking what the water has reached. If a pipe has burst, turn the water off at the stopcock before you call — see the panel on the right for where to look for it.

Taps, valves and showers

Reseating or replacing worn taps, swapping failed isolation and stop valves, and fitting mixer or thermostatic shower valves. Where a tap keeps dripping after a new washer, the seat itself is usually the problem.

Toilets and cisterns

Running overflows, failed flush mechanisms, worn fill valves and leaking pan connectors. A cistern that refills on its own is wasting water continuously, even when nothing is visibly wrong.

Radiators and heating pipework

Replacing radiators and valves, moving them for building work, adding a radiator to an existing circuit where the system will carry it, and fitting thermostatic valves. Cold at the bottom usually means sludge; cold at the top usually means air.

Outside taps and garden supplies

Fitted with the isolation and backflow protection the water regulations require. That protection is there to stop water being drawn back from a hose or a butt into the drinking supply, which is why an outside tap is not simply a tee off the nearest pipe.

Replacing old pipework

Lead and old galvanised steel are worth replacing on sight. Where we find lead supply pipe we will tell you, because the part outside your boundary is usually the water company responsibility rather than yours.

What we do not do

Being straight about the boundaries saves everyone a wasted visit. We do not take on mains water supply replacement beyond the property boundary, sewage treatment plants, or commercial catering installations.

Anything touching the gas supply — a cooker, a gas fire, a boiler — is gas work rather than plumbing, and is covered by our Gas Safe registration. See boiler repair and boiler installation.

Before you call

Two things make almost every plumbing visit shorter, and both are worth doing now rather than during an emergency.

  1. Find your stopcock and check that it turns. A seized stopcock discovered during a burst is a much worse afternoon.
  2. Find the isolation valves under the sink and behind the toilet, if they exist. They let one fitting be worked on without draining the house.

If water is actively coming through a ceiling, turn off the supply and the electrics to that area first, then call. See emergency callout.

Where is my stopcock likely to be?

Most often under the kitchen sink. Failing that, try a downstairs cloakroom, under the stairs, an airing cupboard, or near where the supply pipe enters the house. There is also an external stop valve, usually under a small cover at the boundary.

Why do my radiators need bleeding every year?

Occasional air is normal. Air that keeps coming back usually means it is being drawn in through a small leak or a failing automatic air vent, or that corrosion inside the system is generating gas. Repeated bleeding is a symptom worth investigating rather than a routine chore.

Can you work on a bathroom I am having fitted?

Yes, we take on the plumbing for a bathroom installation. We do not do tiling, plastering or electrical work, so those need a separate trade.

Do you clear blocked drains?

We clear blockages within the property. Anything beyond the boundary is usually the water company responsibility, and we will tell you when that is the case.
