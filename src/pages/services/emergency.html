Emergency callout

Some emergencies are ours. One of them is not — and getting that order right matters more than anything else on this page.

Call {{site.phone.display}}

If you smell gas, call this number first

Do not call us. Call the National Gas Emergency Service on 0800 111 999. It is free, it is staffed 24 hours, and it is the correct first call for any gas escape anywhere in Great Britain.

  1. Open the windows and doors.
  2. Do not touch light switches, plugs or anything else electrical, and do not use a mobile phone inside the building. Do not light a match or anything else.
  3. Turn the gas off at the meter, if you can reach it safely.
  4. Leave the property.
  5. Call 0800 111 999 from outside.

The same applies if your carbon monoxide alarm sounds, or if anyone in the house has the symptoms of carbon monoxide exposure — headache, dizziness, nausea or breathlessness that improves when they go outside. Get everyone out first, then call.

If anyone has those symptoms, get medical help as well as reporting the escape. Call 999 if someone has collapsed, is confused or is struggling to breathe. Otherwise contact NHS 111 or your GP the same day, and tell them you suspect carbon monoxide — it changes what they test for.

Once the supply has been made safe, the appliance still has to be put right. That is the point at which to call us.

What we treat as an emergency

Anything actively causing damage, or leaving a household without heating or water in conditions where that is a real problem.

  • A burst pipe, or water coming through a ceiling
  • A leak that cannot be isolated at a valve
  • No heating or hot water in cold weather, or where someone is vulnerable to cold
  • A boiler leaking water onto electrics
  • An appliance disconnected as unsafe that you need restored

What to do while you wait

For a burst or a major leak

  1. Turn the water off at the stopcock — usually under the kitchen sink, sometimes in a cloakroom, under the stairs, or where the supply pipe enters the house.
  2. Open the cold taps downstairs to drain the system down.
  3. If water is anywhere near electrics, turn off the power to that area at the consumer unit, provided you can reach it safely and without standing in water.
  4. Move what you can out of the way and contain the rest.

For a leaking boiler

  1. Turn the boiler off.
  2. Turn off the water supply to it if there is a valve you can reach.
  3. Do not keep topping up the pressure to keep it running.

What is not an emergency

A boiler that has stopped in mild weather, a dripping tap, a radiator that is cold at the bottom, or an error code on a system that is otherwise working. These are worth booking as a normal visit — see boiler repair or general plumbing — and you will usually pay less for them than for an emergency callout.

If you are not sure which you have, call and describe it. Working that out is our job, not yours.

Is a boiler with no heating an emergency?

Usually not, in the sense of needing someone that hour — unless the property is occupied by someone vulnerable to cold, or there is a risk of pipes freezing. Tell us when you book either way, because it changes how we prioritise the visit.
