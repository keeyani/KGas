Landlord gas safety records

The annual check the law requires on every gas appliance in a rented property, and the record you have to give your tenant.

What the law requires

If you let a property with gas appliances, you have three duties that run every year, and they are yours rather than your tenant's.

  1. Every gas appliance and flue in the property must be checked for safety every twelve months by a Gas Safe registered engineer.
  2. Gas pipework, appliances and flues must be maintained in a safe condition.
  3. You must give the tenant a copy of the record within 28 days of the check, and give a new tenant a copy at the start of the tenancy.

The document is officially a landlord gas safety record. Almost everyone calls it a CP12, after the form it used to be issued on. They are the same thing.

The duty covers appliances you provide. An appliance the tenant owns is generally outside it, though the flue and the connecting pipework serving it are not. We will check a tenant-owned appliance on request, charged as an additional appliance.

What the check covers

  • Each appliance checked for gas tightness and correct operation
  • Flues checked to confirm the products of combustion go outside
  • Ventilation passages checked as clear and adequate
  • Safety devices tested, and the gas installation pipework checked
  • The record issued, listing every appliance and the result for each

A safety check is not a service

This is the point landlords most often get caught out on. The safety check confirms that the appliances and flues are safe at the time of inspection. It is not maintenance, and passing it does not mean the boiler has been serviced.

Where a boiler is still under a manufacturer warranty, that warranty usually depends on an annual service, which is a separate piece of work. The two are commonly done on one visit — see annual servicing. Booked together, the check and the service are {{site.pricing.landlordBundle}} rather than {{site.pricing.landlordSeparate}} separately.

Timing and renewals

A check can be carried out in the two months before the current record expires without shortening the twelve-month cycle — the new expiry date still runs from the old one. That gives you a window to arrange access rather than a single deadline.

We keep the expiry date on file and contact you two months before it, so there is time to arrange access.

If the tenant will not give access

The duty to have the check done still applies, so the thing that matters is being able to show you took reasonable steps. Keep a record of every attempt: letters sent, appointments offered, and any response.

Explaining to a tenant that the check is a safety measure for their benefit generally works better than citing the tenancy agreement. Where it does not, take advice before forcing the issue — entering without permission carries its own legal risk.

If something fails

An appliance found to be unsafe is classified under the industry danger categories, turned off and labelled, and the record shows the result for each appliance. You will be told what has to happen before it can be used again.

Where a repair is needed, see boiler repair.

Common questions

What does a landlord check cost?

A landlord gas safety check is {{site.pricing.landlordCheck}} for one appliance, plus {{site.pricing.landlordExtraAppliance}} for each additional appliance in the property.

See how our pricing works.

Is a landlord gas safety record the same as a boiler service?

No. The safety check confirms that appliances and flues are safe at the time of inspection. A service is maintenance of the appliance itself. They are commonly done on the same visit, but one does not replace the other.

How often does the check have to be done?

Every twelve months, and the record must reach the tenant within 28 days of the check — or at the start of the tenancy for a new tenant.

What happens if the tenant will not give access?

The duty still applies. Keep a record of every attempt to arrange access, including letters and appointments offered, because demonstrating repeated reasonable attempts is what matters if the check cannot be completed on time.

Do you handle portfolios of several properties?

Yes. For five properties or more we schedule the checks together and invoice monthly.

How do I get the record?

We email the record to you on the day of the check, and leave a copy at the property for the tenant.
